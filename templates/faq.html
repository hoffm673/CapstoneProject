1. What is the difference between Difficulty and Time Commitment?

A class can be time-consuming without being intellectually difficult (e.g., weekly discussion posts, lengthy reading packets, or repetitive busywork). Conversely, a course can have low time commitment but feature extremely abstract or complex mathematical concepts that require deep cognitive effort.

We separate these two metrics so you can distinguish between a course that will take up your entire Saturday afternoon versus one with conceptually demanding exams.

2. How is Difficulty (1–5) Defined?

Difficulty reflects how hard the core concepts and evaluations are to comprehend and pass:

  • Score 1 Trivial Concepts: The material is straightforward, intuitive, and requires minimal cognitive strain. Most students grasp it right away.
  • Score 2 Accessible: Mostly clear concepts with occasional topics requiring extra attention or review before quizzes.
  • Score 3 Moderate / Standard: Introduces new, non-intuitive topics. Requires genuine concentration and studying, but can be reasonably grasped by most students.
  • Score 4 Challenging: In-depth theory, tricky proofs, or heavy problem-solving. Regular tutoring or study groups may be needed.
  • Score 5 Extremely Difficult: Very complex, abstract, or fast-paced material. Students often struggle to keep up and may feel they barely grasped the subject despite significant effort.

3. How is Time Commitment (1–5) Defined?

Time commitment measures the number of hours you must dedicate outside of scheduled lecture time for homework, readings, labs, and projects:

  • Score 1 Negligible: Almost no outside work required beyond attending class. Minimal or quick weekly assignments.
  • Score 2 Light: 1 to 2 hours per week outside of lecture. Standard light readings or short weekly worksheets.
  • Score 3 Standard: Around 3 to 5 hours per week outside lecture. Regular problem sets, coding labs, or weekly quizzes.
  • Score 4 Heavy: 6 to 9 hours weekly. Major semester projects, multi-stage lab reports, or mandatory weekly group meetings.
  • Score 5 Insane Workload: 10+ hours every week outside of class. The class feels like a full-time job with continuous, demanding deliverables.

4. How Does the Course Load Calculator Work?

The Course Load Calculator takes the courses you intend to enroll in for an upcoming semester and aggregates their peer review ratings.

  • It computes your Total Credits.
  • It averages the Difficulty Scores and Time Scores submitted by previous students.
  • It lets you set your personal limits (e.g. keeping difficulty below 3.5) and visualizes whether your intended schedule fits your goals.
  • If you are logged into your account, you can save your schedule so you can revisit and edit it anytime.

5. Why Do I Need a UWM Email to Register?

To maintain authenticity and prevent spam, account creation requires an official @uwm.edu email address. We send a 6-digit verification code to confirm that every reviewer is an active member of the campus community.

Note: You can search for courses and read reviews without an account. An account is only required to submit reviews and save custom schedules.

6. Are My Reviews Anonymous?

Your reviews display your public username and (optionally) your major. Your real name, student ID number, and email address are never displayed publicly.

You can also edit or delete any reviews you have submitted at any time through your My Profile page.

7. Is RateMyUWM Affiliated with UWM Administration?

No. RateMyUWM is an independent student-run capstone project created by UWM students for UWM students. It is not managed, sponsored, or affiliated with the University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee administration or faculty.
